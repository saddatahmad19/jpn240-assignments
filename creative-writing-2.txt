# Creative Writing 2

Higuchi Ichiyo's story, Child's Play, would be the best story to make a film out of. I would keep the main characters and the basic story intact, but I would move the setting from 1890's Tokyo to a crowded city neighborhood in the late 1990's to make it appeal to modern times. By doing this, the story would be more relatable to the audience without losing the main concepts in Ichiyo's story. The neighborhood would still have small businesses, a nightlife district, family money problems, and a strong divide between the kids who have more and the kids who do not. The children would continue to spend most of their time playing together while slowly being pulled into the adult world around them.

The qualities of the main characters would stay mostly the same. Midori would be outgoing, confident, generous, and used to getting attention because of her older sister's position in the nightlife district. Shota would come from a wealthier family and would be friendly and confident, but he would still act like a kid who does not completely understand what growing up means. Nobi would be quite and intelligent, but also uncomfortable with himself and worried about what other people think. Chokichi would be the aggressive leader who cares a lot about pride and winning. Sangoro would be the funny kid who is liked by everyone but is also one of the poorest. That contrast between the characters is important because their friendships are never completely seperate from money, family, background, and reputation.

I would keep the festival as one of the biggest parts of the film. It gives the kids a chance to show off, compete, and forget about everything else for a little while. I would film those scenes with a handheld camera, quick cuts, and very excentric stimuli like loud drums, lights, and lots of movement. It would cater to the whimsy and childlike nature for the characters in the festival. I want the beginning of the movie to feel fun and almost overwhelming, the same way the festival probably feels to the children. Then the tone would slowly change when Chokichi's group attacks Sangoro and then Midori gets hit on the forehead with the muddy sandal, slowing the whole pace of the film down. The audio would pause for a moment, allowing the camera to stay on Midori's face in shock and disbelief. That moment is important to the context of the film as it shows that the children are no longer just playing.
